What your customers see

Every request has a customer side. This is the whole of it — the page where they send you a request, and the private page where they follow it. No app to download, no account to make.

For: office staff & owners Companion to: "Turning a phone call into a request" Draft v3

Two links, and that's it. Your customer's entire experience runs on two web pages:

1. Your request page — one fixed link you share anywhere. Customers fill in a short form to reach you.

2. Their tracking page — a private link created for each request. They watch progress and message you there.

They never sign in. The link is the key — which is why the tracking page tells them to keep it to themselves.

01Your request page

One link. This is what opens when a customer clicks it.

The page carries your business name and, if you've set them, your logo and customer-facing phone. The form is short on purpose — only name and mobile phone are required. Everything else helps you respond faster but won't block them from submitting.

02They describe the problem and submit

Urgency and address are collected here — and kept between you and them.

Below the contact fields, the customer writes what's wrong, picks an urgency (Routine, Soon, or Urgent), and gives the service address. One tap on Submit request and they're done.

i

The address and urgency a customer enters here go to your queue, not onto their tracking page. If they forward that link to a family member, the address doesn't travel with it.

03Their private tracking page

Created the instant they submit. This is the link they keep.

No confirmation email to wait for — the tracking page opens right away. It shows the current status in plain words, the reference code, their original message, and how to reach you. The line up top reminds them the link is private.

04When you post an update, they see it here

Same page, no refresh needed on their part.

Every customer-page update you post from the workbench lands on this page, tagged as the latest. The status heading changes with it — here it moved to "Your request is scheduled" — and the page notes when the last update happened.

05They can reply, ask, or send details back

The customer isn't stuck waiting by the phone.

From the tracking page a customer can send a message, ask for a call, share their availability, add details or a photo, or cancel. Anything they send shows up in that request's communication history on your side.

Where your link comes from

Settings → Public Link & Profile.

Your request-page link is fixed and yours to reuse. Copy it once and put it wherever customers look for you — website, email signature, text replies, invoices, a printed sign.

Worth knowing

  • No account, ever. Customers never make a password. If they lose the tracking link, you can re-send it from the request.
  • The request-page link doesn't change. Share it once. It's safe to print.
  • The tracking link is per-request and private. Anyone who has it can see that one request — that's why the page says to keep it to themselves.
  • The customer only sees what you post. Internal notes, the address, urgency, and your team's activity stay on your side. Only "Customer-page updates" cross over.
  • Their words are preserved. The original message and every reply are kept verbatim in the request history — useful when a job is disputed later.
  • Not for payment details. The message box warns against it. Take card numbers by phone or your normal invoicing, never in Keep.

Questions businesses often ask

About the customer experience, and what to promise.

Do customers need to download an app or create an account?

No. Customers use a normal web link — no username, password, or Ophalo account.

They use two links: your public request-page link to start a request, and their private tracking link to follow that specific request.

What does a customer see when they open our public request link?

A request form branded with your business name and, if configured, your logo and customer-facing phone number.

They provide contact information, describe what they need, select an urgency, and enter the service address. After submitting, they go straight to their private tracking page.

What information is required from a customer?

Name and mobile phone are required, so the business has a reliable way to identify and contact the customer.

Email, preferred contact method, ZIP code, and apartment/unit are optional — unless the customer chooses email as their preferred contact method.

Why do we ask for an address if the customer can't see it later?

The service address helps your team understand where work is needed and respond appropriately. It is deliberately kept off the customer's tracking page.

So if a customer forwards their tracking link, the recipient does not automatically see the service address.

Is the customer's information sold or used for marketing?

No. The request form states that the information is shared only with the business receiving the request, and is not sold or used for marketing.

Can customers choose how we contact them?

Yes — text message, phone call, email, or no preference. This is a helpful instruction for your team, not a guarantee that every communication will use that method.

What do Routine, Soon, and Urgent mean?
  • Routine — work that can be scheduled when convenient.
  • Soon — the customer would like help in the next day or two.
  • Urgent — the customer believes the request needs prompt attention.

Urgency helps your team prioritize. It is not shown on the customer's private tracking page.

Does Urgent mean we provide emergency service?

Not automatically. Urgent highlights the request for the business, but it does not promise an immediate response or emergency dispatch.

For an immediate safety risk, customers should contact emergency services first.

What happens immediately after a customer submits a request?

Their private tracking page opens right away. No email confirmation to wait for, no account to create.

The page shows their request status, reference code, original message, and the business contact information.

Do customers get a notification every time we post an update?

The update appears on their private tracking page. Keep using your normal text, phone, or email process whenever you need to be sure a customer sees something time-sensitive.

Don't promise automatic text or email notifications unless your business has separately confirmed and enabled that process.

What can customers do from the private tracking page?

Follow the request status, read business updates, send a message, ask for a call, share availability, add useful details, or cancel the request when appropriate.

Everything they send is kept with that request's communication history for the business to review.

Can customers see our internal notes, prices, or team activity?

No. Customers see only customer-facing status and updates the business deliberately posts to the tracking page.

Internal notes, staff activity, assignments, urgency, address, and internal financial information stay private to the business.

Can a customer see another customer's request?

No. Each request has its own private tracking link.

Anyone who has that specific link can open that specific request, though — so the customer should keep it private and avoid forwarding it unless they're comfortable sharing the request details.

What if a customer loses their private tracking link?

The business can re-send or share the private link from the request record. The customer does not need an account to recover access.

Can we use the same public request link everywhere?

Yes. It's designed to be reused — website, email signature, text replies, invoices, QR codes, printed materials, social profiles.

What happens if we replace our public request link?

The old link stops working. Only replace it if it has been misused or needs to be retired.

If you replace it, update every place the old link was shared or printed.

Can customers submit more than one request?

Yes. Each submission creates its own request and private tracking page. If the requests are related, your team can review them together internally.

What if a customer submits a duplicate request by mistake?

Your team can compare the name, phone number, description, and service address to spot likely duplicates. Keep preserves every submission so the business decides how to handle it, rather than silently losing a request.

Can a customer cancel a request?

Customers can use their tracking page to tell the business they no longer need help. The business still controls the internal workflow and confirms the appropriate final status.

Can customers send sensitive information through the message box?

They should not send passwords, full card numbers, banking information, or other highly sensitive details. The page reminds them of this.

Collect payment details through your normal secure process — by phone, or your existing invoicing/payment system.

Does Ophalo replace our phone, text, email, or scheduling system?

No. Ophalo gives each request a clear place to start, follow updates, and keep the customer/business conversation connected to that request. Keep using the phone, text, email, calendar, and payment tools you already rely on.

What should we tell customers before we share the link?
"Use this link to send us the details. After you submit it, you'll get a private page where you can follow updates and message us — no account needed."
What should our team avoid promising?

Avoid promising:

  • A specific response time, unless your business has committed to one.
  • Immediate emergency service.
  • Automatic text or email notifications for every update.
  • Customer-visible pricing, appointments, or payment processing, unless those workflows are separately confirmed.
  • That a customer message alone confirms a booking or scheduled visit.

The safest language: "We received your request," "We'll review it," and "Check your private page for updates."
