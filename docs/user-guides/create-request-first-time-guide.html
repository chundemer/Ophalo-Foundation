Turning a phone call into a request

When a customer calls, texts, or walks in with a problem, the first thing you do in Keep is log a request. This walks you through it two ways — texting the customer a link, or typing the details in yourself.

For: office & dispatch staff Time: ~2 minutes per request Draft v3 · staff-entered requests

What is a "request"? A request is one customer problem you're on the hook for — "furnace won't turn on," "need a quote for a new AC." Keep gives it a short code (like UVD2TCA3), a customer page the homeowner can watch, and a running history so nothing gets lost between the call and the truck.

You don't need every detail to start. A name, a phone number, and one line about the problem is enough — you can fill in the rest later.

01Start a new request

Top-right of the screen, from anywhere in Keep.

Click + New Request in the top navigation bar. It opens a panel on the right side of the screen. This is the same button whether you're on the request queue, a customer's page, or the Getting Started screen.

02Pick how you're capturing it

Two ways in — use whichever fits the call.

The panel opens on the Text a Link option first. If the customer is on the phone and you'd rather they describe the problem themselves, use this. If you already have the details and just want them recorded, switch to typing it in.

Track A Text the customer a link

Keep builds a text to the customer with a link to a short form. You scan a QR code to send it from your own phone, then the customer describes the problem themselves. Good when you want their words on record.

Track B Type it in yourself

You fill in the customer and the problem while you're on the call. Fastest when you already know what they need. This is the everyday path.

Track A · Text a link

A1

Enter the customer's mobile number

Type their number into the field and click Prepare text. This builds a text message addressed to that number with the request link already written in — it doesn't send anything yet.

A2

Scan the code with your own phone

Keep shows a QR code. Scan it with your phone's camera. Your messaging app opens with the text to the customer already written — link and all. Check it and hit send.

i

Keep works this way on purpose: the text goes out from your phone, not an automated texting service, so there's no per-message cost. Give the message a quick look before you send.

After the customer sends the text and fills in the short form, the request appears in your queue automatically.

Track B · Type it in yourself

B1

Look up the customer by phone

Click Enter request for customer, then type the customer's phone number. Keep checks it against people you've helped before — if there's a match, it fills their details in for you. If not, you'll enter them fresh on the next screen.

B2

Fill in the request

Name and a one-line description are the only things you truly need. Pick a Source so you know later how this came in. Add the service address if you have it — it saves a step for whoever gets dispatched. Then click Capture Request.

03Share the customer page

The request exists now — but the customer can't see it yet.

After you capture, Keep confirms with the new request's code. To let the customer follow along, send them their page link: scan the QR code with your phone to text it (same as step A2), or use Email customer page link. You can share now or from the request later.

!

Until you send that link, the request is private to your team. Capturing a request never notifies the customer on its own.

04Where the request lives now

Every request has one home: its workbench.

This is where the whole job plays out — contacting the customer, recording work, posting updates, marking it done. New requests start as Received and show up in your queue under Open Work. Keep also tells you the next sensible step; for a request you haven't shared yet, that's Share Link.

Worth knowing

  • You can't break anything by starting one. If a call turns out to be nothing, the request can be cancelled. Better to log it and close it than lose it.
  • Texts send from your phone. The QR code hands a ready-made message to your own phone — Keep doesn't send texts for you. So always give the message a quick look before you hit send.
  • The customer sees nothing until you share the page. Capturing a request is private to your team.
  • One problem, one request. If a customer has two unrelated issues, make two requests. It keeps the history and the schedule clean.
  • Returning customers fill themselves in. Always start Track B with the phone number — if you've helped them before, the name and details drop in automatically.
  • The short code is yours to use. Read it out on the phone, write it on a work order — anyone on the team can pull the request up by it.
